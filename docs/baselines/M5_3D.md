# M5 reliable 3D geometry and core machining

In progress since 2026-10-24. This file records the evidence for each M5 task as it lands.

## D01: Productionize the selected mesher

I05 selected explicit Rhino-generated meshes with a host-side exporter instead of a standalone B-rep mesher. D01 implements that decision end to end: the exporter records its claims on the mesh, the importer measures the mesh against the solid it claims to represent, and CAM refuses meshes whose measured deviation exceeds the operation tolerance.

### Implementation

- `scripts/rhino/warpnine_export_meshes.py` (Rhino 8, RhinoCommon): meshes selected or all B-reps, extrusions, surfaces and SubDs. For B-rep sources it meshes, measures in both directions with `Brep.ClosestPoint`/`Mesh.ClosestPoint`, and halves the mesher tolerance until the exporter's own measurement meets the request (Rhino's `Tolerance` does not bound the chord error along curved trim edges). It writes user strings `warpnine.source`, `warpnine.source_type`, `warpnine.tolerance`, `warpnine.exporter`, `warpnine.revision` (SHA-1 of the source geometry JSON), `warpnine.face_ranges` (B-rep face to mesh-face ranges), `warpnine.mesher_tolerance` and `warpnine.exporter_measured`. `scripts/rhino/warpnine_make_m5_fixture.py` builds the acceptance solid and runs the exporter unattended.
- `crates/warp-import-3dm/native/qualify.{h,cpp}`: independent measurement. Source to mesh: trimmed surface samples (strict trim inclusion by sampled trim loops) and 3D edge samples, projected onto a uniform triangle grid. Mesh to source: vertices and centroids projected by Newton iteration onto the nearest candidate faces (best seed per face from a 12×12 seed grid per face); a projection outside the face trims falls back to the trim-edge distance and is counted as an outside-trim sample, which is how filled holes and overshoot are caught. Extrusions and surfaces are measured through their B-rep form. SubD sources are measured against the level-3 subdivided limit polyhedron (`ON_SubD::GlobalSubdivide` + `SurfacePoint`), and the result carries a note that the reference itself is approximate. Budgets: 40 000 source samples, 20 000 mesh samples.
- `native/bridge.cpp`: reads the claims, emits `provenance` on meshes and `represented_by` on their solids, enforces the triangle memory limit and polls the cancellation flag between objects and inside long loops. `warp3dm_read(path, tolerance, max_triangles, cancel, ok)`.
- `crates/warp-core/src/mesh.rs`: `MeshProvenance`, `MeasuredDeviation`, `FaceRange`, `MeshQuality`; `Mesh::analyze` (welded edge classification: boundary, non-manifold, inconsistent winding, degenerate, signed volume), `Mesh::transformed` (reverses winding under mirroring transforms), `Mesh::signed_volume`. `Geometry::Unavailable` gained `represented_by`.
- `crates/warp-import-3dm/src/lib.rs`: qualification policy. A claim is honoured when the measurement meets it within the source model tolerance (`solid_represented`); otherwise the solid stays unavailable with the measured reason (`mesh_unqualified`). Unmeasurable sources stay claimed-only (`mesh_unmeasured`). `mesh_topology`, `mesh_source_missing`, `mesh_source_unsupported` and `multiple_meshes` diagnostics. `ImportOptions` has `max_triangles` (default 2 000 000) and `cancel`.
- `crates/warp-cam/src/document.rs`: selecting a represented solid as a part surface machines its mesh; a measured deviation above the operation tolerance refuses generation with the measured value; `AllEligible` is no longer blocked by represented solids; meshes are placed with `Mesh::transformed`, so mirrored block instances keep outward normals.
- `crates/warp-app`: imports run on a worker thread with a Cancel button, elapsed time and a mesh-triangle limit control; the browser shows each mesh's topology summary and provenance (red when unqualified or not oriented); the import report lists machining meshes.

### Tests

- `warp-core` `mesh::tests`: closed box orientation, mirrored transform keeps positive volume (a naive vertex map inverts it), open/flipped/degenerate/non-manifold detection, provenance claim comparison and legacy JSON compatibility.
- `warp-import-3dm` `tests/import.rs`:
  - fixture variant 9: sphere (128×64 inscribed polyhedron, radius 10), planar annulus with hole (128-gon), capped extrusion, smooth SubD box, and a closed box mesh in a mirrored block. Expectations are geometric: sphere deviation between 0.003 and 0.0105 mm, annulus between 0.002 and 0.0035 mm (128-gon sagitta 0.0030), extrusion under 0.0105, SubD under 0.5 with a limit-polyhedron note; all four solids represented; mirrored box volume +64 at x 116–120; selecting the solid resolves 16 128 triangles; tolerance 0.002 refuses; AllEligible at 0.02 refuses because of the SubD mesh and passes at 1.0.
  - fixture variant 10: 12×6 sphere (measured 0.5–0.8 mm, expected sagitta 0.68), disc filling the annulus hole (mesh-to-source 2.9–3.05 mm with outside-trim samples), control-net box for the SubD, orphan claim, flipped mesh (one inconsistent edge); all three solids stay unavailable with "Re-mesh" reasons; AllEligible part surfaces are blocked.
  - cancellation flag set before reading fails with "cancelled"; `max_triangles` 1000 fails with "memory limit"; 100 000 imports.
  - `fixtures/rhino-m5/sculpted-v8.3dm`: produced by Rhino 8 through the exporter (wavy NURBS top, offset solid, through hole). See the measured result below.
- `warp-cam` `tests/document.rs`: `qualified_meshes_represent_solids_and_mirrored_instances_stay_outward`.

### Restrictions

- Measurements are sampled maxima (40 000 source and 20 000 mesh samples), not proven bounds. Trim loops are sampled with 96 points per trim and edges with 256 points; the inclusion band absorbs that chord error for mesh projections.
- SubD references are the subdivided limit polyhedron; their own deviation from the true limit surface is not bounded, and the exporter's SubD density is a subdivision level rather than a tolerance.
- Import cancellation is cooperative: the native reader checks between objects and every 4096 triangles or 1024 samples, so a cancel can take a fraction of a second on large files. The memory limit counts triangles, not bytes.
- Face provenance is recorded as triangle ranges per source face; edge provenance is implied by adjacent face ranges only.
- Meshes without provenance (STL, plain Rhino meshes) keep working with unknown accuracy; the browser labels them as such and generation does not refuse them.
- Rhino is required to create meshes from `.3dm` solids. Users without Rhino import STEP instead (D01b below). The fixture script was run against Rhino 8 on this machine and exits by itself.

## D01b: STEP import (Open CASCADE)

A `.3dm` solid without a Rhino-exported machining mesh stays unmachinable, which blocks anyone who receives Rhino files but has no Rhino licence. D01b adds STEP import on a geometry kernel WarpNine controls, so the exact B-rep is meshed and qualified inside the importer. Open CASCADE Technology 7.9.3 (LGPL 2.1 with the OCCT exception) is pinned by commit in `vendor/occt-pin.txt`, fetched and built once by `scripts/fetch-occt.ps1` as static libraries (foundation, modelling data and algorithms, mesh, STEP; no visualization, OCAF or third-party dependencies), and linked by the crate's `build.rs`.

### Implementation

- `crates/warp-import-step/native/bridge.cpp`: `STEPControl_Reader` reads the file in millimetres; every solid, free shell and free face becomes an object. Names come from the STEP representation items (`MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP`, `SHELL_BASED_SURFACE_MODEL`), falling back to the product name and then `Solid N`. Each shape is meshed with `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` at the import tolerance and measured in both directions: source-to-mesh from trimmed surface samples (`BRepTopAdaptor_FClass2d`, strict) and 257-point 3D edge samples against a uniform triangle grid; mesh-to-source from vertices and centroids projected onto their own face by `ShapeAnalysis_Surface`, with a tolerance band on the trims and the 3D-edge polyline distance for samples beyond it. The deflection is halved up to four times until the measurement meets the request or the triangle budget is reached; the best attempt is kept and the shortfall recorded as a note. The source model tolerance is the maximum shape tolerance reported by `ShapeAnalysis_ShapeTolerance`.
- `crates/warp-import-step/src/lib.rs`: `StepImporter` implements `GeometryImporter`; native calls are serialised behind a process-wide mutex because Open CASCADE keeps global reader state. Format name `STEP AP203|AP214|AP242` from the file schema.
- `crates/warp-core/src/interchange.rs`: the namespacing, provenance and mesh-qualification policy moved out of the Rhino crate into `finish_native_import`, shared by both importers (`ImportFormat` carries the format name and the re-mesh hint). `ImportResult.format` replaces the hard-coded "Rhino" summary.
- `crates/warp-app`: one import entry (`import_model`) for `.3dm`, `.step` and `.stp`, on the File menu, the geometry browser and the command line (`warpnine part.step`). Reimport lists both kinds of sources.
- Fixtures: `warpstep_write_fixture` writes synthetic STEP (variant 0: radius-10 sphere, 20 x 30 x 15 box with a radius-4 through hole, open interpolated wavy face; variant 1: a 1 x 2 x 0.5 inch box written in inches). `scripts/rhino/warpnine_make_step_fixtures.py` exported the M5 sculpted solid and its top face from Rhino 8 as `fixtures/rhino-m5/sculpted-v8.step` (AP203, 109 kB) and `sculpted-top-surface.step` (6 kB).

### Tests (`crates/warp-import-step/tests/import.rs`)

- Synthetic solids: sphere volume within 0.5% of 4/3 pi r^3 and measurably curved; box-minus-hole volume within 0.5% of 9000 - 240 pi with seven face ranges; wavy face open, corners at z = 0, sampled peaks of +-2.853 reached, overshoot bounded; every mesh closed and outward where the source is a solid, every deviation within the request plus the source tolerance, zero outside-trim samples, distinct names, provenance exporter `warpnine-occt/7.9.3`.
- Inch file: 25.4 x 50.8 x 12.7 mm box of 12 triangles, exact volume, `source_units` INCH, scale 25.4.
- Tolerance and CAM: 0.2 mm import yields at least four times fewer sphere triangles with a measured deviation between 0.01 and 0.2; selecting the solid as a part surface machines its mesh; an operation tolerance below the measured deviation refuses generation; `AllEligible` includes every represented object.
- Cancellation, the triangle memory limit, a non-STEP file and an invalid tolerance fail with their messages.
- Rhino fixture: the STEP solid is named `Sculpted solid`, meshed closed and oriented, qualified against 0.01 mm, and its volume agrees with the Rhino-exported, independently qualified 3dm machining mesh within 0.1%; the top-face file imports as an open `surface` named `Sculpted top` whose top matches the solid, and it resolves as a part surface.

### Restrictions

- Assemblies are flattened: each placed solid becomes one object, with no block definitions or instances. Colours, layers and PMI are ignored.
- Names depend on the exporter writing them into the representation items; Rhino 8 and the Open CASCADE writer do, with the product name as the fallback.
- Measurement budgets and sampling caveats are the same as D01. Meshing itself (`BRepMesh`) is not cancellable mid-face; cancellation is observed between faces and inside the measurement loops.
- Only Windows/MSVC is qualified, like the openNURBS bridge. The one-time Open CASCADE build takes 20-40 minutes and about 530 MB.
- The exact B-rep is discarded after import; D02's surface queries work on the qualified mesh. Keeping the B-rep for exact queries is a possible later extension.

## D02: Surface query services

The Z-map rasterises the mesh to a grid and drops the tool onto grid nodes, so walls, fins and edges are resolved only to the cell size and the error is unrelated to the operation tolerance: on a block with a vertical wall at x = 6.1 mm and a 0.25 mm grid, a 2 mm flat tool over-cut the wall by 0.227 mm. D02 adds exact cutter-contact queries on the qualified mesh behind one interface, keeps the Z-map for roughing, and measures the remaining path error instead of inferring it from a step size.

### Implementation

- `crates/warp-cam/src/surface.rs`:
  - `Cutter` is the tool profile grown by the stock to leave (swept horizontally by the wall allowance, lowered by the floor allowance; the same convention as the Z-map kernel).
  - `SurfaceQuery` is the interface (`drop`: lowest non-gouging tip height at a point; `penetration`). `MeshSurface` implements it exactly: triangles sit in a uniform XY bin index (CSR layout, one visit per triangle per query); the tip height is the maximum over facet and edge contacts. Facet contact uses the closed-form `Cutter::contact_radius` (where the profile slope reaches the facet slope, or the rim of the segment that cannot) and is valid only when the contact point lies on the facet; edge contact maximises a concave function along the edge by golden-section search with the end points evaluated exactly. Every supported profile is convex and non-decreasing in the radius, which is what makes both searches exact. Candidates are sorted by an upper bound (triangle top over the nearest footprint distance) and pruned against the running best. `ZMapSurface` adapts the existing grid to the same interface.
  - `Sampling` and `drop_polyline` sample a 2D path: chords no longer than `min(resolution, cutter reach)` so no feature narrower than the footprint falls between samples, then each chord is split until the exact contact at its midpoint lies within half the operation tolerance (3D distance) of the chord or the chord is shorter than that half. At a vertical step the chord's XY length bounds the lateral overshoot, so the minimum step is also half the tolerance. The residual of chords that could not be split further is returned, never assumed to be zero.
  - `cl_map` fills a level map with exact contacts for waterline and roughing; `gouge_distance` bounds the 3D distance from a tip position to the contact surface (vertical gap, a local-plane estimate from four probes, a 32-direction probe at a caller's ceiling, then a directional scan with bisection; within 0.5% for a planar wall and never below the true distance); `path_deviation` accumulates the gouge bound and the vertical material left along a 3D polyline.
- `crates/warp-cam/src/ops.rs`: `SurfaceMode { Auto, Exact, ZMap }` on `Common` (serde default `Auto`, so saved documents load unchanged). `Auto` resolves to exact contact for Parallel, Spiral, Radial, Project Curves and Waterline and to the Z-map for 3D Roughing. `resolution` is now the surface sampling step (longest chord, or the grid cell).
- `crates/warp-cam/src/ops3d.rs`: the setup owns the chosen query; runs are sampled in parallel and simplified within the other half of the tolerance; level operations take their map from `cl_map`.
- `crates/warp-app/src/cam_ui.rs`: "Surface sampling" and a "Surface queries" selector on 3D operations.

### Tests

- Unit (`surface.rs`): contacts on a 30 degree plane match closed forms for flat, ball, bull-nose, 90 degree V with a tip flat, 150 degree V, tapered ball, and grown flat and ball cutters; nothing under the footprint is unbounded; refinement on a plane needs no residual and respects the step.
- `crates/warp-cam/tests/surface.rs`:
  - Brute-force oracle: triangles sampled densely (0.02 mm on a stepped block with vertical walls and a 0.05 mm fin, 0.08 mm on a faceted dome); for five tool shapes and 40 random points each, the exact contact is never below any sampled contact and exceeds the brute force by less than 0.3 mm.
  - Stepped block, 2 mm flat tool, parallel finish at 0.25 mm sampling and 0.01 mm tolerance: an independent penetration oracle (mesh points inside the tool body, distance to the profile curve) measures 0.0038 mm in exact mode and 0.227 mm in Z-map mode; exact mode machines both plateaus at their heights up to a radius from the wall and the fin.
  - Dome, 6 mm ball: penetration 0.0020 mm, gouge bound 0.0020 mm, no material left beyond 0.012 mm vertically.
  - Sampling finds the 0.05 mm fin's 2.05 mm contact band from a 0.25 mm step (edges within 0.005 mm), reports zero residual, and caps the step at the footprint radius.
  - Legacy operations without a `surface` field load as `Auto`; `Auto` resolves per operation kind.
- `crates/warp-import-step/tests/import.rs`: the Rhino-exported STEP solid (17 182 triangles) finished with a 6 mm ball at 1 mm stepover: exact mode stays within the 0.01 mm tolerance of the contact surface (gouge bound 0.0085 mm, walls included); the Z-map at 0.1 mm gouges 0.078 mm.

### Restrictions

- Gouge and material-left figures are sampled (at most the given spacing apart and at least four points per chord). The 3D gouge bound is an upper bound at every sampled point, within 0.5% for planar walls; nothing is proven between samples. Swept checks with shank and holder are D05.
- Material left is measured vertically; at a wall it reads the step height and is not meaningful there.
- Exact-mode cost grows with the triangles under the footprint; large tools on dense meshes are slower than the Z-map (0.58 s against 0.12 s on the Rhino solid). 3D roughing therefore stays on the Z-map by default, and the Z-map remains selectable; its error is measured in the tests, not refused.
- Waterline and roughing contours still interpolate linearly between level-map nodes at `resolution`, even when the nodes are exact contacts. Exact cut levels are D03.
- The error budget of a finished part adds the mesh's measured deviation from its source (D01) to the operation tolerance met here and to post rounding; the three are measured with different distance definitions and are only summed, not combined more tightly.

## D02a: Z-map roughing cost

Reported 2026-10-25: 3D roughing of a 93 x 75 mm solid (`test.warp`, 12.7 mm flat, 0.1 mm sampling, 17 levels) took 11 s in the release build with every core busy, and the same job with a 50 mm V-bit from the user's library ran for minutes. Measured with `cargo run --release -p warp-cam --example time_project -- test.warp`: the Z-map cutter-location map took 10.4 s of the 11 s. Its cost is `cells x footprint samples`: 1.2 M cells x 11 300 samples for the 12.7 mm tool at 0.1 mm, and 785 000 samples per drop for the 50 mm V-bit. Both factors grow with the square of `1 / step`, so halving the sampling step costs 16x.

### Implementation

- `crates/warp-cam/src/zmap.rs`: the footprint `Kernel` is stored by rows (`KernelRow { dj, di0, h, flat }`). `cl_map` applies one footprint row at a time against contiguous map rows; a row whose profile height is constant (every row of a flat or face mill, single-sample rows of any tool) is a sliding-window maximum computed with block prefix and suffix maxima (van Herk / Gil-Werman), so a flat tool costs about `3 x cells x rows` instead of `cells x samples` and no longer depends on the tool radius. Other rows take a contiguous direct loop. `drop_tool` uses the same rows. `ZMapWork::estimate` predicts the cost from the tool, step, radial stock and extent without building the kernel.
- `crates/warp-cam/src/ops3d.rs`: roughing and waterline extract the level contours of the map for all levels in parallel.
- `crates/warp-cam/src/ops.rs`: `default_resolution_for(kind)`: new 3D roughing operations start at 0.25 mm sampling; finishing operations keep 0.1 mm. Existing operations keep their saved value.
- `crates/warp-app/src/cam_ui.rs`: the 3D section shows a "Z-map work" line (`cells x samples`, or `cells x rows` for flat tools, labelled fast / slow / very slow at 2e10 and 2e11 predicted sample visits) whenever the operation resolves to the Z-map, a "Default" button beside the sampling step, and the generation time next to the move count. The status line reports the generation time too.
- `crates/warp-cam/examples/time_project.rs` times every operation of a project (optional step and tool overrides); `crates/warp-cam/examples/kernel_bench.rs` times the map per tool shape on a synthetic surface or a project's mesh.

### Tests

- `zmap::tests::cl_map_matches_the_plain_maximum_for_every_tool_shape`: on a random 37 x 29 map with a spike and a hole, the windowed map and `drop_tool` equal the plain maximum over every footprint offset (to 1e-12) for flat, ball, bull nose, V and face mills, with and without stock to leave; the flat tool really takes the windowed path and the ball does not.
- `zmap::tests::work_estimate_counts_the_real_kernel`: the estimate's samples and rows equal the built kernel's for four tool and step combinations, its cell count is within a row and a column of the padded map, and halving the step multiplies the predicted work by 16 for a ball and 8 for a flat tool.
- The existing `warp-cam` surface and STEP finishing tests still pass with the measured Z-map error figures unchanged (the map is the same numbers, computed differently).

### Restrictions

- Non-flat tools still pay `cells x samples`; the sampling step is the control, and the Z-map work readout exists so the choice is informed rather than a surprise. Bull-nose rows are flat only on their central part, which the row-wise flat test does not exploit.
- The estimate predicts visits, not seconds: about 3 G visits/s for non-flat rows and 50 to 60 G/s for flat rows on the test machine. The labels are thresholds on visits.
- Coarser Z-map steps resolve walls more coarsely (see the D02 measured result); roughing should carry stock to leave of at least the step. D03 makes the cut levels exact.
